# **SUMMARY OF ARGUMENTS**

# **Issue 1**

**Whether The Present Pil Is Maintainable Before This Hon\`Ble Court?**

This Public Interest Litigation (PIL) is a misuse of judicial process, aiming to interfere with an ongoing criminal case already under legal adjudication. It lacks substantial public interest, as it concerns an individual dispute, not a systemic issue affecting society. The petitioner has no legitimate standing, and the matter can be addressed through existing legal remedies. PILs are meant for public interest, not personal grievances or political motives. Entertaining this PIL would lead to judicial chaos, setting a dangerous precedent for frivolous litigation. The Hon’ble Court must dismiss this PIL to preserve the integrity of PIL jurisprudence and judicial efficiency.

# **Issue 2**

### **Whether This Case Falls Under the Doctrine of Rarest Of The Rare Were Accused To Be Punished With The Death Penalty?**

The case does not meet the "rarest of rare" criteria for the death penalty due to a lack of substantial evidence linking the accused to the crime. The prosecution has failed to provide conclusive proof, such as forensic evidence or reliable witness testimony. The victim's statements are inconsistent, and there is no corroboration to support the claims. Additionally, there is no proof of the accused's presence at the crime scene. Given these deficiencies, the case does not justify capital punishment, and imposing the death penalty would violate fundamental legal principles of fair trial and justice.

# **Issue 3**

### **Whether The Five Accomplices Should Also Be Convicted for Section 302 As Well As 376d Of Ipc Along With Main Perpetrator?**

The respondents argue that the five accomplices should not be convicted under Sections 302 and 376D IPC due to lack of substantial evidence. They emphasize the double presumption of innocence in acquittal, absence of direct proof, gaps in circumstantial evidence, procedural lapses, inconclusive medical reports, and failure to establish common intention. The prosecution’s inability

to prove essential ingredients of the crimes beyond reasonable doubt justifies upholding the trial court’s acquittal.

# **Issue 4**

### **Whether The Political Influence Exerted By Shitaljeet Choba To Manipulate The Investigation Process Undermine The Integrity Of The Judicial Process And Constitute An Abuse Of Power Under The Prevention Of Corruption Act Read With S 3 And 4 Of The Prevention Of Money Laundering Act?**

The Respondent argues that Shitaljeet Choba, as a political analyst, is not a public servant under the Prevention of Corruption Act. The Enforcement Directorate (ED) failed to follow due process under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, making the arrest illegal. There is no evidence of illegal funds or bribery, and the prosecution has not proven criminal conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt. Hence, no manipulation of the investigation or abuse of power occurred, warranting acquittal.

1. This present Public Interest Litigation (PIL) is wholly untenable, legally unsustainable, and an abuse of judicial process. It is an attempt to circumvent due process, bypass established legal remedies, and mislead the Hon’ble High Court into interfering in an individual criminal matter already pending before the judicial forums.

2. PIL is a powerful constitutional remedy, but its sanctity must be preserved. The courts have consistently warned against its misuse for political agendas, media sensationalism, and private disputes disguised as public interest.
