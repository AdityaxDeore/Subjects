Introduction

A UML 2.0 Use Case Diagram captures the functional requirements of a system from the perspective of external entities. It establishes system boundaries, identifies interactions, and defines behavioral dependencies without detailing internal programmatic code. The Hospital Management System automates clinical and administrative workflows, bridging patient registration, consultation, diagnostics, room allocation, and billing.

2. System Boundary & Use Cases

A Use Case represents a specific piece of functionality (e.g., Admit Patient, Pay Fee) and is drawn as an ellipse. The System Boundary is drawn as a rectangle enclosing all use cases, indicating the scope of the software. Actors remain outside this boundary.

3. Basic Associations

Associations represent a communication path between an Actor and a Use Case. They are drawn as solid, plain lines. Note that arrows are rarely used here unless demonstrating direction of data flow; a plain line is standard.

4. The <<include>> Relationship

An <<include>> relationship means a Use Case absolutely requires the functionality of another Use Case to complete its job. It is depicted as a dashed arrow pointing from the base Use Case to the included Use Case. For example, Admitting a Patient always includes Generating a Patient ID.

5. The <<extend>> Relationship

An <<extend>> relationship indicates optional or conditional behavior. It is depicted as a dashed arrow pointing from the extending (optional) Use Case to the base Use Case. For example, if no rooms are available during allotment, the "No Room Available" flow extends the "Allot Room" process.

7. Use Case Specifications

Below are detailed textual descriptions outlining the normal flow and preconditions for the core use cases identified in the diagram.

UC-01: Admit Patient

Actors: Receptionist, Patient
Description: Registers a new patient into the hospital system.
Pre-condition: Receptionist is authenticated into the system.
Normal Flow:
  1. Receptionist opens registration form.
  2. Enters patient's personal and medical history details.
  3. System executes the <<include>> relationship to Generate Patient ID.
  4. Record is successfully saved.

UC-02: Allot Room

Actors: Receptionist
Description: Assigns an available inpatient bed to an admitted patient.
Pre-condition: Patient must be formally admitted to the hospital.
Normal Flow:
  1. Receptionist queries ward availability.
  2. Selects bed and assigns it to Patient ID.
  3. System updates bed status to occupied.
Alternate Flow: If no beds are available, the system triggers the <<extend>> relationship to No Room Available workflow, adding the patient to a waitlist.

UC-03: Schedule Diagnosis

Actors: Doctor, Patient
Description: Doctor consults the patient, examines symptoms, and records clinical findings.
Pre-condition: Patient has a valid appointment or is admitted in the ward.
Normal Flow:
  1. Doctor retrieves patient file.
  2. Doctor logs examination notes.
  3. System forces the doctor to execute <<include>> Prescribe Medicine based on findings.
  4. Consultation is finalized and saved.

UC-04: Pay Fee

Actors: Receptionist, Patient
Description: Settlement of all outstanding hospital bills.
Pre-condition: Services (consultation, room, labs) must be rendered and logged.
Normal Flow:
  1. System consolidates all charges associated with the Patient ID.
  2. Itemized invoice is presented to the patient.
  3. Patient pays the fee via cash or digital transaction.
  4. Receptionist records payment; system generates receipt.
