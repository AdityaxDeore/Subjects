Study of SDLC Models

Aim

To study and analyze different Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) models such as Waterfall, Agile, Spiral, and V-Model, understand their characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages, and choose the most suitable SDLC model for different software projects.

Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

  • A structured process used for planning, designing, developing, testing, deploying, and maintaining software applications.
  • Provides a systematic approach to software development.
  • Ensures that high-quality software is delivered reliably within budget and strictly on time.

1. Waterfall Model

Introduction

  • One of the earliest and simplest SDLC models, following a strict linear and sequential approach.
  • Each phase of software development must be completely finished before the next phase begins.
  • The output of one phase acts as the direct input for the subsequent phase, flowing downwards like a waterfall.
  • Highly suitable for projects where requirements are completely defined, well-understood, and unlikely to change.
  • Extensive documentation is maintained at every single stage to simplify project management.

Diagram

flowchart TD Req[Requirement Analysis] --> Design[System Design] Design --> Dev[Implementation] Dev --> Test[Testing] Test --> Deploy[Deployment] Deploy --> Maint[Maintenance]
Figure 1.1: Waterfall Model Phases

Characteristics (Features)

  • Follows a linear and sequential software development process.
  • Each phase must be completed before the next phase begins.
  • Requirement analysis is completed before development starts.
  • Proper documentation is maintained throughout the project.
  • Customer involvement is mainly during the requirement gathering phase.
  • Testing is performed only after the coding phase is completed.

Advantages

  • Simple and easy to understand and implement.
  • Well-defined phases make project management easier.
  • Proper documentation helps in future maintenance.
  • Easy to estimate project cost, schedule, and resources.
  • Works incredibly well for small and medium-sized projects.

Disadvantages

  • Difficult to accommodate changes once development begins.
  • Customer involvement is severely limited after the initial phase.
  • Errors discovered late can be extremely expensive to fix.
  • Working software is only available at the very end of the cycle.

Applications

  • Library Management System
  • Student Management System
  • Payroll Management System
  • Inventory Management System
  • Banking Record Management System

Example Project: Library Management System

  • Stable Requirements: Features such as book issue/return and fine calculation rarely change.
  • Clear Sequence: The project follows a fixed sequence of phases without the need for frequent revisions.
  • Organized Development: Proper documentation at each step ensures an organized and highly efficient process.

2. Agile Model

Introduction

  • An iterative and incremental model that focuses heavily on flexibility, collaboration, and continuous improvement.
  • The project is divided into small, manageable parts called iterations or sprints.
  • Each sprint is designed to deliver a fully working version of the software.
  • Encourages continuous customer involvement and immediate feedback throughout the entire development process.
  • Highly adaptable, allowing developers to quickly respond to suddenly changing market requirements.

Diagram

flowchart LR Plan((Plan)) --> Design((Design)) Design --> Develop((Develop)) Develop --> Test((Test)) Test --> Release((Release)) Release --> Feedback((Feedback)) Feedback --> Plan
Figure 2.1: Agile Iterative Cycle

Characteristics (Features)

  • Follows an iterative and incremental development approach.
  • Development is divided into small iterations called sprints.
  • Delivers working software at the end of each sprint.
  • Encourages continuous customer involvement and feedback.
  • Easily accommodates changing requirements during development.
  • Team members work collaboratively with regular communication.

Advantages

  • Highly flexible and adaptable to rapidly changing requirements.
  • Delivers working software quickly through short development cycles.
  • Continuous customer feedback significantly improves product quality.
  • Allows for early detection and resolution of software defects.
  • Reduces overall project risks through frequent testing and reviews.

Disadvantages

  • Difficult to estimate the total project cost and completion time upfront.
  • Requires highly experienced and skilled development teams.
  • Continuous customer participation is absolutely necessary for success.
  • Documentation may be less detailed compared to traditional models.

Applications

  • Social Media Applications
  • E-commerce Websites
  • Mobile Applications
  • Online Food Delivery Systems
  • Ride-Sharing Applications

Example Project: Social Media Application (Instagram Clone)

  • Dynamic Requirements: User expectations and market trends change frequently.
  • Continuous Updates: Features such as Stories, Reels, and Messaging require constant iteration.
  • Rapid Feedback: Agile enables developers to release features in short sprints and gather immediate user feedback.
  • High Adaptability: Ensures the application maintains quality while meeting rapidly changing expectations.

3. Spiral Model

Introduction

  • A risk-driven SDLC model introduced by Barry Boehm in 1986.
  • Combines the structured features of the Waterfall Model with the iterative nature of the Prototyping Model.
  • Development is carried out in a series of repeated cycles called spirals.
  • Each spiral strictly focuses on planning, risk analysis, development, and customer evaluation.
  • Primarily designed to identify, analyze, and minimize risks at every single stage of software development.

Diagram

flowchart TD P[Planning] --> R[Risk Analysis] R --> E[Engineering & Development] E --> C[Customer Evaluation] C --> P
Figure 3.1: Spiral Model Cycle

Characteristics (Features)

  • Follows an iterative and heavily risk-driven software development approach.
  • Development is carried out in multiple loops known as spirals.
  • Risk identification and mitigation are performed before any coding begins.
  • Customer feedback is collected immediately after each iteration.
  • Combines the advantages of both the Waterfall and Prototyping models.

Advantages

  • Provides excellent risk management through continuous, dedicated risk analysis phases.
  • Highly suitable for massive, complex, and mission-critical software projects.
  • Changes in requirements can be easily and safely incorporated.
  • Reduces the chances of catastrophic project failure by identifying risks early.

Disadvantages

  • Overall development cost is extremely high due to repeated iterations.
  • Requires specialized experts in risk analysis and project management.
  • Far more complex to understand and implement than traditional SDLC models.
  • Development time may increase significantly because of multiple cycles.

Applications

  • Hospital Management System
  • Air Traffic Control System
  • Aerospace and Defence Software
  • Banking and Financial Systems
  • Medical Software Systems

Example Project: Hospital Management System

  • High Complexity: Manages patient records, billing, pharmacy, and medical history.
  • Risk Mitigation: Identifies and minimizes critical risks related to data security and system performance.
  • Iterative Safety: Each cycle ensures sensitive patient information is secured before proceeding.
  • Continuous Improvement: Feedback from hospital staff is incorporated to ensure the system remains reliable.

4. V-Model (Verification and Validation)

Introduction

  • An advanced extension of the Waterfall Model where every development phase has a corresponding testing phase.
  • Testing activities are proactively planned and performed alongside each exact development stage.
  • Designed in a V-shaped structure: the left side is development, the right side is corresponding testing.
  • Ensures defects are identified very early, drastically improving the overall quality of the software.
  • Widely considered the best model for projects requiring ultimate reliability, security, and accuracy.

Diagram

flowchart TD Req[Requirement Analysis] --> Sys[System Design] Sys --> Arch[Architecture Design] Arch --> Mod[Module Design] Mod --> Code((Coding)) Code --> UT[Unit Testing] UT --> IT[Integration Testing] IT --> ST[System Testing] ST --> AT[Acceptance Testing] Req -.-> AT Sys -.-> ST Arch -.-> IT Mod -.-> UT
Figure 4.1: V-Model Structure

Characteristics (Features)

  • Follows a strictly sequential and highly structured software development process.
  • Every development phase is directly linked to a corresponding testing phase.
  • Emphasizes both verification (building it right) and validation (building the right thing).
  • Testing activities are meticulously planned from the very beginning of the project.

Advantages

  • Early testing planning helps detect defects long before deployment.
  • Produces exceptionally high-quality and reliable software.
  • Reduces the immense cost of fixing critical defects in later stages.
  • Highly suitable for safety-critical and security-sensitive applications.
  • Ensures complete traceability between development code and testing activities.

Disadvantages

  • Extremely difficult to accommodate changing requirements once testing plans are set.
  • Far less flexible than iterative models such as Agile or Spiral.
  • Working software is only available after the entire V-cycle is completed.
  • Can be overly time-consuming for highly dynamic, fast-paced projects.

Applications

  • Online Banking Systems
  • ATM Software
  • Railway Reservation Systems
  • Defence and Military Software
  • Aerospace Applications

Example Project: Online Banking System

  • High Security Need: Handles sensitive financial data, fund transfers, and online payments.
  • Parallel Testing: Every development phase is followed by a rigorous corresponding testing phase.
  • Defect Prevention: Early testing actively identifies security vulnerabilities and transaction errors.
  • Guaranteed Reliability: Ensures the resulting banking system is secure, legally compliant, and accurate.

Justification for Selecting SDLC Models

The selection of an appropriate SDLC model is a crucial step. Every project has different requirements, complexities, and risk factors. Choosing the right development model improves quality, reduces risks, and ensures timely delivery.

1. Library Management System – Waterfall Model

  • Project Overview: Automates catalog management, member registration, and book issues.
  • Requirement Stability: Functional requirements are clearly defined upfront and remain highly stable.
  • Development Approach: Follows a highly structured, sequential workflow without the need to revisit phases.
  • Cost Efficiency: Because revisions are rare, it is an extremely cost-effective and time-efficient model.

2. Social Media Application – Agile Model

  • Dynamic Ecosystem: Features like Stories, Reels, and Messaging constantly evolve based on market trends.
  • Iterative Sprints: Developers release functional versions of the application in short, manageable sprints.
  • Continuous Feedback: Customer feedback is immediately incorporated without breaking the entire system architecture.
  • High Flexibility: Provides the supreme adaptability needed for rapid, modern software deployment.

3. Hospital Management System – Spiral Model

  • Mission-Critical Data: Manages confidential medical history, billing, and life-saving emergency services.
  • Risk Mitigation: Focuses heavily on identifying risks like data breaches and performance bottlenecks early.
  • Iterative Evaluation: Each module (e.g., pharmacy, billing) is developed, rigorously tested, and evaluated independently.
  • System Reliability: Massively reduces the possibility of catastrophic system failures in a healthcare environment.

4. Online Banking System – V-Model

  • Ultimate Security: Performs critical financial transactions where even a minor defect can cause severe financial loss.
  • Quality Assurance: Emphasizes strict QA from day one, associating testing phases with every single development stage.
  • Multi-Level Verification: Utilizes Unit, Integration, System, and Acceptance testing to guarantee accuracy.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Supports the strict documentation and traceability required by international banking laws.

Comparison of SDLC Models for an Online Banking System

SDLC Model Reason for Not Selecting the Model
Waterfall Model Testing begins only after the development phase is completed, delaying the detection of critical security vulnerabilities. This makes transaction errors vastly more expensive to fix.
Agile Model Focuses on rapid development and changing requirements. However, banking apps require complete verification and regulatory approval before deployment, making rapid sprints too risky.
Spiral Model Provides excellent risk analysis, but repeated development cycles heavily increase project cost and time. The complex looping process is less beneficial than structured sequential validation.

Why the V-Model is the Best Choice for an Online Banking System

  • Highest Quality Assurance: Integrates testing activities simultaneously with every single stage of software development.
  • Rigorous Verification: Ensures that each development phase produces the exact correct output according to strict specifications.
  • Sensitive Data Handling: Comprehensive testing is absolutely essential due to the highly sensitive financial information involved.
  • Early Defect Detection: Reduces catastrophic software failures, improves system reliability, and ensures compliance with strict banking regulations.

Conclusion

  • Every SDLC model possesses highly unique characteristics, distinct strengths, and specific functional limitations.
  • The Waterfall Model excels for projects with fixed, well-defined requirements.
  • The Agile Model is ideal for modern applications requiring continuous updates and immediate customer feedback.
  • The Spiral Model is preferred for massive, complex systems where risk management is the highest priority.
  • The V-Model is the ultimate choice for security-critical, quality-sensitive applications like banking and aerospace.
  • Selecting the correct SDLC model is fundamentally critical to improving software quality and delivering reliable applications within budget.
