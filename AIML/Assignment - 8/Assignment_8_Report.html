1. Aim

To implement a Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier for analyzing the sentiment (Positive/Negative) of Amazon smartphone reviews and to gain insights into brand performance based on customer feedback.

2. Problem Statement

Classify Amazon cell phone reviews into Positive or Negative sentiment using Naive Bayes. Then, utilize the classification results to analyze which brands have the highest positive sentiment, the most negative reviews, and identify words most associated with negative reviews.

3. Objectives

  • Understand conditional probability and the Naive Bayes independence assumption.
  • Merge Amazon items and reviews datasets and preprocess review text using TF-IDF.
  • Train a Multinomial Naive Bayes model to classify sentiments (Positive vs Negative).
  • Evaluate classifier accuracy, precision, recall, and a confusion matrix.
  • Perform brand-level analysis to answer key questions regarding customer satisfaction and complaints.

4. Dataset Description

The experiment utilizes the Kaggle dataset "Amazon Cell Phones Reviews". This dataset offers a rich collection of customer feedback focused strictly on smartphones across ten major global brands including Apple, Samsung, OnePlus, and Xiaomi.

Dataset Composition

The data is split into two primary files:

  • items.csv: Contains product metadata like ASIN (Amazon Standard Identification Number), Brand, Price, and Total Reviews.
  • reviews.csv: Contains the actual customer reviews, including Star Ratings (1 to 5), Review Titles, and the Body text.

By mapping the ratings, we create our target sentiment classes:

Positive Sentiment Pos4 or 5 star ratings

5. Theory and Mathematical Formulation

For a document \(d\) and sentiment class \(c\), Naive Bayes estimates the probability:

\(P(c \mid d) \propto P(c)\prod_{i=1}^{n}P(w_i\mid c)\)

The model assumes that words contribute independently once the sentiment is known. We utilize TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) rather than raw word counts to reduce the impact of overly common words (like "the" or "and").

Calculation Example: TF-IDF & Laplace Smoothing

1. TF-IDF Calculation:
\( \text{TF}(w) = \frac{\text{Frequency of word } w \text{ in review}}{\text{Total words in review}} \)
\( \text{IDF}(w) = \log\left(\frac{\text{Total Reviews}}{\text{Reviews containing word } w}\right) \)
\( \text{Score} = \text{TF} \times \text{IDF} \)

2. Laplace Smoothing:
To prevent an unseen word from making the entire probability zero, we add a smoothing parameter \(\alpha\):
\(P(w\mid c)=\frac{N_{wc}+\alpha}{N_c+\alpha V}\)
Where \(V\) is the vocabulary size, and \(\alpha=0.10\) in our implementation.

6. Methodology & Workflow

The sentiment analysis pipeline follows a structured, sequential process:

1. Data Ingestion
Merge items.csv and reviews.csv using ASIN
↓
2. Preprocessing & Labeling
Filter 3-star ratings, Map 4-5 to Positive, 1-2 to Negative
↓
3. Feature Extraction
TF-IDF Vectorization (Unigrams & Bigrams)
↓
4. Model Training
Fit Multinomial Naive Bayes (\(\alpha=0.1\))
↓
5. Business Intelligence
Brand Analysis & Negative Keyword Extraction

7. Algorithm

1. Load items.csv and reviews.csv and merge them.
2. Extract Sentiment labels from ratings and drop Neutral (3-star) reviews.
3. Split data into training and testing sets.
4. Fit TF-IDF vectorizer on training reviews.
5. Train Multinomial Naive Bayes on the transformed text features.
6. Predict sentiment for test documents and compute classification metrics.
7. Group predictions by brand to find highest positive sentiment and most complaints.
8. Extract words most associated with negative reviews using log probabilities.

10. Conclusion

Multinomial Naive Bayes effectively classifies Amazon smartphone reviews into positive and negative sentiments, providing a robust baseline.
